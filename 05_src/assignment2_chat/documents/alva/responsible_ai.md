# Responsible AI in the Workplace

**Knowledge base:** Alva — AI Workforce Readiness Advisor  
**Primary source(s):** Microsoft Learn  
**Document type:** Independently written educational synthesis (not official course text)  
**Compiled:** 2026-11-17

## Core considerations
Responsible AI includes attention to privacy, safety, security, fairness, transparency, and human accountability. Employees should not confuse convincing language with verified facts; AI-generated material may be inaccurate or reflect biases. Practices should be proportional to the stakes of the task.

## Practical controls
Use only approved tools and data. Remove personally identifying and confidential material from examples unless appropriate authorization and protections are in place. Check important claims against trustworthy sources. Review for exclusionary or unfair effects when AI informs hiring, performance evaluation, or access to opportunities. Explain when AI contributed to an output if that context matters to its users.

## Leadership responsibilities
Define who approves uses, who checks outputs, and how employees can flag errors or harmful suggestions. Maintain oversight rather than delegating consequential judgments to an unverified model. Provide training that includes realistic failure cases and what to do when the system is unsure.

## Questions for Alva
- What could go wrong if the output is mistaken or biased?
- Which data are prohibited from entering the tool?
- Who is accountable for the final decision, and how is it documented?

## Boundary
This summary is educational; it does not replace organizational policy, legal advice, or an evaluation of a specific AI system.
