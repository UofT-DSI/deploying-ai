## Core concepts
Generative AI can produce text and other content in response to prompts. Large language models work with patterns learned during training; their fluent responses should not be treated as verified evidence. A prompt supplies an instruction, relevant context, and often constraints about desired output. An AI agent may combine model reasoning with tools, but additional autonomy increases the importance of boundaries and monitoring.

## Workforce applications
Examples include drafting a first version of a message, brainstorming training activities, summarizing approved internal material, and transforming a rough outline into a clearer document. Outputs should be checked for factual errors, unsuitable claims, and missing context. Employees should avoid sharing confidential or personal information in tools unless the organization explicitly authorizes that use.

## Readiness considerations
Before deployment, define permitted use cases, training needs, review responsibility, and an escalation route for uncertain results. A successful rollout depends on knowing how to evaluate outputs, not just how to type prompts.

## Questions for Alva
- Is the task primarily drafting, extracting, comparing, or making a consequential decision?
- What source material can the model use, and how will the employee verify the result?
- Does a simple assistant meet the need, or would tool-using automation create unacceptable risk?
