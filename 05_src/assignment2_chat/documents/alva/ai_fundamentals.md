## Core concepts
Artificial intelligence (AI) describes systems that perform tasks involving capabilities such as language processing, recognizing patterns, predicting outcomes, or generating content. These systems differ in what they do: some classify or predict using learned relationships; generative systems create new text, images, or other outputs in response to instructions.

AI does not automatically make an output correct or appropriate. Its usefulness depends on the task, the quality of available information, and human review. A workplace readiness conversation should therefore start with a specific task and a realistic goal, rather than assuming that all activities require AI.

## Workplace implications
Managers can map recurring tasks into categories: information retrieval, drafting, analysis, decisions, and interactions with people. AI may help with parts of those workflows, but high-impact judgments and sensitive processes require accountable human oversight. Teams need basic vocabulary, hands-on practice, and a shared understanding of when not to use an AI system.

## Questions for Alva to explore
- Which tasks consume the most time and are suitable for low-risk experimentation?
- What AI concepts do employees need before using a proposed tool?
- Who validates outputs and remains accountable for decisions?

## Practical starting point
Choose one low-risk task, define an acceptable output, try the tool using non-sensitive information, and compare the result against an existing manual process. Discuss accuracy, effort, and limitations before scaling.
