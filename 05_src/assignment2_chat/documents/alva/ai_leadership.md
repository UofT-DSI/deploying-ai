# Leadership for AI Workforce Readiness

**Compiled:** 2026-11-17

## Leadership responsibilities
AI workforce readiness requires leaders to connect technology choices to specific business needs while supporting employee learning and trust. Prosci frames AI transformation as a change to processes, culture, and how people work, not simply the purchase of tools. Microsoft Learn describes productivity opportunities alongside security, compliance, and responsible-use concerns.

## Practices for leaders
Communicate what the organization intends to improve and how outcomes will be measured. Involve employees in selecting and evaluating use cases. Provide role-specific learning opportunities and visible support for experimentation. Set clear boundaries for confidential data, consequential decisions, and output verification. Explain which person remains accountable when AI contributes to a decision.

## A first-month plan (illustrative)
Week 1: Listen to staff and identify one low-risk workflow. Week 2: Clarify permissible data and quality checks. Week 3: Run an optional supported pilot with a few employees. Week 4: Review evidence of usefulness, errors, confidence, and barriers, then decide whether to adjust or expand.

## Questions for Alva
- How does the leader's AI vision connect to frontline tasks?
- What would employees need to feel informed and supported?
- Who owns training, governance, and feedback?
- What evidence will justify scaling or stopping the pilot?
