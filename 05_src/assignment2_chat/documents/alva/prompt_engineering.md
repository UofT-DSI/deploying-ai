# Prompt Engineering for Workplace Tasks

**Knowledge base:** Alva — AI Workforce Readiness Advisor  
**Primary source(s):** Microsoft Learn and OpenAI  
**Document type:** Independently written educational synthesis (not official course text)  
**Compiled:** 2026-11-17

## Core approach
An effective prompt explains the task clearly, gives relevant context, specifies the intended audience, and identifies format and constraints. Prompting is iterative: inspect the first result, identify what was missing, and refine the request. Prompts do not guarantee accuracy, so results must still be checked.

## Practical template
Task: What should the assistant do? Context: What background is relevant and safe to share? Audience: Who will use the output? Format: What structure and length are desired? Constraints: What should be excluded or treated as uncertain? Review: What evidence or criteria will be used to assess the result?

## Workplace example
Instead of 'make an AI training plan,' ask: 'Draft a one-page introductory AI training agenda for customer-service supervisors with no technical background. Include three learning objectives, a practical exercise using non-sensitive examples, and a short section on checking AI outputs. Flag assumptions rather than inventing company policies.'

## Coaching tips for Alva
Help the learner focus a broad request, add appropriate context, and request a revised answer when the first output is incomplete. Encourage human review and avoid placing confidential material in an unapproved tool.

## Common pitfalls
Vague goals, overloaded requests, missing audience context, and treating a polished response as validated evidence can reduce the usefulness of AI-generated output.
